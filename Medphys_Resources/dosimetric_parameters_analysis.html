Central Axis Dosimetric Parameters

Fundamental Radiation Dosimetry Measurements & Analysis

Percentage Depth Dose (PDD)
PDD(d) = [D(d) / D(dmax)] × 100%
  • Definition: Dose at depth d relative to dose at maximum buildup depth
  • Reference: Normalized to dmax (100%)
  • SSD Dependence: Varies with source-surface distance
  • Energy Dependence: Higher energy = deeper penetration
  • Field Size Effect: Larger fields show less dose falloff
  • Applications: Manual calculations, isodose planning
Tissue Phantom Ratio (TPR)
TPR(d,rd) = D(d,rd) / D(dref,rd)
  • Definition: Dose ratio at two depths with same field size at depth
  • SSD Independent: Eliminates inverse square law effects
  • Reference Depth: Usually 10 cm or 5 cm water
  • Field Size: Defined at measurement depth (rd)
  • Stability: Independent of distance variations
  • Modern Use: Preferred for treatment planning systems
Tissue Maximum Ratio (TMR)
TMR(d,rd) = D(d,rd) / D(dmax,rd)
  • Definition: Dose at depth d relative to dose at dmax
  • Reference Point: Always normalized to dmax
  • Phantom Measurement: Requires full scattering conditions
  • Field Definition: Field size at measurement depth
  • Geometric Independence: No SSD dependence
  • Clinical Use: Isocentric treatment planning
Tissue Air Ratio (TAR)
TAR(d,rd) = Dtissue(d,rd) / Dair(d,rd)
  • Definition: Dose in tissue relative to dose in air at same point
  • Measurement: Tissue vs. air comparison
  • Physical Meaning: Accounts for attenuation and scatter
  • Distance Independence: No inverse square dependence
  • Field Size: Defined at measurement point
  • Historical Use: Fundamental dosimetric concept
Scatter Air Ratio (SAR)
SAR(d,rd) = TAR(d,rd) - TAR(d,0)
  • Definition: Scattered radiation component only
  • Derivation: TAR minus primary beam component
  • Physical Meaning: Pure scatter contribution
  • Field Size Dependence: Increases with field size
  • Clarkson Method: Fundamental for irregular field calculations
  • Planning Use: Scatter dose modeling
Scatter Phantom Ratio (SPR)
SPR(d,rd) = Sc(rd) × TMR(d,rd)
  • Definition: Combines collimator and phantom scatter
  • Components: Collimator scatter × tissue scatter
  • Practical Use: Modern treatment planning systems
  • Separation: Isolates different scatter sources
  • Measurement: Often derived from other parameters
  • Accuracy: More precise for complex calculations

Measurement Methodologies & Setup Requirements

TPR/TMR Measurements

  • Setup: Isocentric geometry (SAD = 100 cm)
  • Phantom: Large water phantom (minimum 40×40×40 cm)
  • Detector: Small volume ion chamber preferred
  • Reference: TPR uses 10 cm depth, TMR uses dmax
  • Field Definition: At measurement depth, not at surface
  • Scatter Conditions: Full lateral electronic equilibrium

TAR/SAR Derivation

  • Method: Usually derived from TPR/TMR data
  • Air Measurement: Small field, minimal scatter
  • Tissue Measurement: Full scatter conditions
  • SAR Calculation: TAR minus primary component
  • Validation: Independent scatter measurements
  • Quality Check: Energy independence verification

Clinical Applications & Modern Usage

Treatment Planning Systems

TMR/TPR: Primary data for dose calculation algorithms including pencil beam, collapsed cone, and Monte Carlo methods. Modern TPS use these parameters for accurate dose modeling in heterogeneous media.

Manual Dose Calculations

PDD/TAR: Historical importance for hand calculations and independent dose verification. Still used for quick estimates and educational purposes in medical physics training.

Quality Assurance

All Parameters: Essential for commissioning new equipment, verifying dose calculation accuracy, and performing periodic QA checks on treatment planning systems.

Algorithm Development

SAR/SPR: Fundamental for developing new dose calculation algorithms, particularly for irregular field calculations and scatter modeling in modern treatment techniques.

Field Size Dependence Analysis

Small Field Effects

  • 📏 Field Size < 3×3 cm:
  • • Lateral electronic disequilibrium
  • • Detector volume averaging effects
  • • Source occlusion by collimators
  • • Requires specialized detectors

Large Field Effects

  • 📐 Field Size > 20×20 cm:
  • • Maximum scatter contribution
  • • Flattening filter effects
  • • Collimator scatter saturation
  • • Head scatter considerations

Practical Measurement Considerations

Detector Selection

Ion Chambers: Gold standard for absolute dosimetry, energy independence, excellent for reference conditions.
Diodes: High spatial resolution, real-time readout, energy dependent.
Diamond Detectors: Radiation hard, small volume, minimal energy dependence.

Measurement Uncertainties

Statistical: ±0.5-1% for ion chamber measurements
Systematic: ±1-2% including setup, calibration
Total Uncertainty: ±1.5-2.5% for clinical measurements
Quality Control: Regular calibration and intercomparison required

Modern Automation

Water Scanning Systems: Automated 3D dose measurement
Real-time Analysis: Immediate data processing and QA
Integration: Direct TPS data import and validation
Efficiency: Reduced measurement time and human error

Special Considerations

Temperature/Pressure: Corrections for ion chamber measurements
Phantom Material: Water equivalent materials verification
Beam Quality: Regular TPR20,10 measurements
Setup Geometry: Precise mechanical alignment critical
